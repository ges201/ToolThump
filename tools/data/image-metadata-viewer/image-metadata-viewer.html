▸What is Image Metadata, Anyway?

Think of an image file not as a single object, but as a container. Inside, right alongside the pixels that make up the photo, is a collection of hidden data that tells the story behind the image. This is its metadata.

This data isn't just one thing; it's often a mix of different standards, each with a specific job. The most common types you'll encounter are:

• EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format): This is your camera's automatic logbook. It records the technical details—camera model, lens, shutter speed, aperture, and often, the 'when' (timestamp) and 'where' (precise GPS location).

• IPTC (International Press Telecommunications Council): This is the standard for professional cataloging. It holds descriptive information added by a person, like captions, keywords, copyright notices, and creator details.

• XMP (Extensible Metadata Platform): A more modern and flexible standard created by Adobe. It can store a vast range of information, including data from EXIF and IPTC, plus things like edit history and star ratings.

For a photographer, this combined data is a goldmine for reviewing settings and organizing archives. For everyone else, it can be a major privacy issue. That cute photo of your dog in the garden could be broadcasting your home address via GPS data. That picture of a product you're selling online could contain your name in the copyright field.

Being aware of this hidden data isn't about being paranoid; it's about making smart, informed decisions. This tool exists to pull back the curtain, decoding all these different formats and presenting them in one clear view, so you know exactly what story your photos are telling before you share them.
